Knee Awareness

Sensitive knees mean we pull traditional forward lunges from the program entirely. Step-ups, leg press, and goblet squats are safer and equally effective for quad development. All knee-loading movements use controlled eccentrics — 3-count lower on everything. If a movement causes pain (not just effort), skip it and note it for your next session.

01
Upper / Lower Split
Each muscle group trained twice per week at moderate volume. This is the optimal frequency for hypertrophy at your training age — more effective than a body part split for intermediate trainees. OTF on non-lifting days keeps cardiovascular capacity high without compromising recovery.
02
Controlled Eccentrics
3-count lowering phase on every isolation movement. This is the single highest-leverage technique for creating visible definition without adding more sets. The eccentric creates more micro-damage and growth signal than the concentric. Slow it down — especially on curls, extensions, and raises.
03
Progressive Overload
Beat last session on at least one lift per workout — one more rep, 5 more lbs, or a cleaner set at the same load. Log every session. Without a log, you're guessing. With a log, you're building. This is non-negotiable regardless of how busy life gets.
04
Age-Smart Recovery
At 45, recovery isn't a weakness — it's part of the program. The OTF sessions count toward total weekly load. We're building a 6-day weekly stimulus across 2 OTF + 4 lifting days, with one full rest day. Sleep and protein are your most powerful anabolic tools right now.
05
Compound First, Isolation Second
Every session opens with a multi-joint compound movement while the nervous system is fresh. Isolation work follows. This sequencing maximizes strength gains on the movements that drive the most total muscle growth. Never reverse this order.
06
Protein Is The Priority
100–120g/day is the right target for your bodyweight and goals. Without adequate protein, the training signal can't convert to visible change. Front-load at breakfast and post-workout. You don't need to track every macro — just hit protein and let everything else follow.

Beyond the Gym Floor
Cardio Options & Recovery
OTF Context

Your two OTF sessions per week (Tread50 + 3G) already provide substantial cardiovascular stimulus — roughly 45–55 min of mixed-zone cardio each. That's sufficient. These supplemental options are for active recovery, extra calorie burn on easy days, or if you want to add a light session. They are not required to hit your definition goals — the lifting is doing that work.

Zone 2 Walk
20–40 min easy walk — conversational pace, no breathlessness. Best active recovery tool available. Drives fat oxidation without eating into muscle recovery. Do this on rest day and any day you feel sluggish.
As needed
Incline Treadmill
20–25 min at 3–4% grade, 3.0–3.5 mph. Same benefits as walking but slightly elevated calorie burn. Lower impact on joints than running. Great for days between lifts when you want some extra output without taxing the legs.
1–2x / week
Stationary Bike
20–30 min low intensity (RPE 4–5). Zero knee impact. Perfect option on lower body days as a warm-up, or as a standalone recovery session after a hard lifting week. Peloton muscle memory will transfer directly.
Optional
Rowing Machine
15–20 min steady state. Full body, low knee stress. Excellent upper body active recovery. Keep stroke rate at 22–24 — this is not a race. Focus on the drive pattern: legs, then back, then arms.
Optional
Recovery Protocol
Sleep, Stress & HRV
At 45, recovery IS the program

Muscle definition happens during recovery, not during the workout. The training session is just the signal. Sleep quality, protein intake, and stress management determine how well your body responds to that signal.

Priority order: Sleep 7–8h → Hit protein → Complete sessions → Add cardio.

If you're running low on sleep or high on life stress, reduce session volume by 30% rather than skipping entirely. A shorter session still creates the stimulus. A skipped session creates nothing.

Nutrition Framework
Your Anchor · Based on Your InBody

At 143.1 lb with 61.3 lb of skeletal muscle and a 6-day weekly training load (4 gym + 2 OTF), your protein floor is 120g/day — not 100. The difference matters: at your muscle mass and output level, 100g undershoots what's needed to support recomposition. You don't need to track every macro. Hit protein consistently, eat mostly whole foods, and let the training do its job.

120+
Protein · grams/day
Non-negotiable floor. 30–40g per meal across 3–4 eating occasions. Front-load at breakfast — don't try to catch up at dinner. Eggs, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, chicken, fish, protein shakes as needed.
Fiber
25g / day target
Supports satiety, gut health, and body composition. Vegetables, legumes, and whole grains. A high-fiber tortilla (like Mission Carb Balance) at lunch is one of the easiest 10g fiber adds available.
Time
Meal Timing
Pre-workout: protein + carbs 1–2 hours before. Post-workout: 30–40g protein within 2 hours. Pre-bed: slow-digesting protein (casein, cottage cheese) if you wake up hungry or training volume is high.
80/20
Food Quality Rule
80% whole foods — lean protein, vegetables, whole grains, healthy fats. 20% flexible for adherence and sanity. Perfection is the enemy of consistency at this stage of the journey.
Supplement Stack
Core Recommendations
Whey or Casein Protein
Use to bridge gaps in whole food protein intake. Whey post-workout (fast), casein pre-bed (slow). Not required if hitting 120g+ from food alone — but a useful tool when life gets busy.
Core
Creatine Monohydrate
5g daily, any time of day. Most evidence-backed supplement for strength and muscle gains. No loading phase needed. Mix and drink immediately if using with acidic liquid.
Core
Omega-3 Fish Oil
2–3g EPA+DHA daily with food. Anti-inflammatory, supports muscle protein synthesis and joint health. Especially relevant with high weekly training volume and knee sensitivity.
Core
Magnesium (Glycinate or Malate)
300–400mg before bed. Sleep quality and muscle recovery. Most people with high training volume are mildly deficient. Moon Juice Magnesi-Om or any glycinate form works well.
Core
Vitamin D3
2000–4000 IU daily if not already getting sun exposure. Test levels if possible — deficiency blunts recovery and mood. If already on Rx D3, skip the OTC version.
Situational
Caffeine (Pre-Workout)
100–200mg 30 min before morning or early afternoon sessions. Do not use within 6 hours of bedtime — sleep quality is more important than any pre-workout effect.

Brooke's 4-Week Roadmap
Week-by-Week Protocol
How to Use This Section

Each week has a specific focus and set of adjustments layered onto the base program. The workout days (A, B, C, D) stay the same every week — what changes is the intensity, tempo, and load targets. Log every session. At the end of Week 4, do a new InBody scan and reassess before building the next block.

Week 01
Orientation
Days 1–7 · RPE cap: 6–7
This week is entirely about learning the gym and finding your numbers. Every machine is different — Anytime Fitness equipment won't feel like BBC. Don't chase load.

Focus: Complete all 4 sessions. Find a starting weight for every exercise where you finish each set with 3 reps clearly in reserve. Log it all — weight, reps, how it felt.

Tempo: No specific eccentric tempo yet. Just move with control. Feel the muscle you're supposed to be working.

Skip the finishers this week. Build the habit first.
Week 02
Establish
Days 8–14 · RPE cap: 7–8
Now you know where things are. This week add the 3-count eccentric to every isolation movement — curls, lateral raises, tricep extensions, kickbacks. This single change will make familiar weights feel brand new.

Load: Add 5 lb to any barbell or machine movement where Week 1 felt easy (RPE under 6). Keep everything else the same.

Finishers: Add them back in on Days A and B. Skip C and D finishers one more week.

Check-in: How are the knees? If step-ups are causing discomfort, swap to leg press foot-high variation.
Week 03
Load
Days 15–21 · RPE target: 7–8 all sets
Progressive overload week. Every compound lift goes up 5 lb if Week 2 felt manageable. Add the full 3-count eccentric + 1-second pause at peak contraction on hip thrusts and curls specifically.

All finishers active on all 4 days this week.

Superset pace: Minimize rest between A1 and A2, B1 and B2. The metabolic stress from tight supersets is part of the definition stimulus.

Protein check: Are you consistently hitting 120g? Week 3 is when training stress peaks — under-eating here blunts adaptation directly.
Week 04
Consolidate
Days 22–28 · RPE: 7 · volume hold
Intentional back-off week. Same exercises, same structure — but drop volume by 20% (3 sets instead of 4 on compounds) and reduce load by 10%. This is not a rest week; it's a consolidation week. Your body uses this window to cement the adaptations from Weeks 1–3.

Focus on quality: Perfect form on every rep. This is the week to film a few sets and watch your own movement.

Skip finishers this week.

End of Week 4: Schedule your next InBody scan. Take front, side, and back progress photos. Compare to your starting point — 4 weeks of consistent work will show.
After Week 4 · What Comes Next

The 4-week block establishes the foundation. If you're feeling strong and consistent, the next block (Weeks 5–8) introduces drop sets on the final round of each superset, increases compound loads another 5–10 lb, and adds a fifth set to hip thrusts and RDLs.

If life got in the way and consistency was broken — repeat this block. There's no shame in extending the foundation phase. The program only works when you actually do it.

Session Logging — Do This Every Time

Exercise · Weight Used · Sets Completed · Reps Per Set · RPE (1–10) · Any notes (knee felt fine, left shoulder tight, etc.)

Notes app, paper, whatever you'll actually use. The log is your coach between sessions. Without it, you're guessing. With it, you always know exactly what to beat next time.
